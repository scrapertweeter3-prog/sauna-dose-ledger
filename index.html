The dose is four sessions above 175, not the heater

A sauna protocol on record calls for four sessions a week above 175 degrees. The heat is not the dose, the count is. Owning the heater bills nothing by itself, and a session that never clears the bar is spent electricity, not protocol. The ledger that matters is cost per counted session, and it splits into two prices: the dollars and the waiting.

Price the counted session first. A 6 kilowatt home heater running 45 minutes of preheat plus a 30 minute session burns 7.5 kilowatt hours per use. At 18 cents a kilowatt hour, one dosed session costs $1.35 in power. Run the full protocol, four a week, 208 sessions a year, 1,560 kilowatt hours, $280.80 a year. Per decade that is $2,808, less than a single year of most supplement stacks.

The waiting is the real price tag. Forty five of every seventy five minutes are preheat, and 208 sessions a year turns that into 9,360 minutes, 156 hours a year of standing in front of a warm box. Money is the small line here. A protocol dose is paid in patience, and most owners quit the patience long before the power bill hurts.

The casual lane shows what skipping the bar costs. Two relaxed sessions a week that cruise at 150 degrees burn the same 7.5 kilowatt hours each, 104 sessions, $140.40 a year for zero counted doses. Half the spend of the protocol and none of it registers, because below 175 the minimum dose is untouched. Warm is not dosed.

Dropping sessions costs proportionally. Three a week instead of four is 156 counted chances instead of 208, a quarter of the dose left on the table every year. The protocol does not grade effort, it grades temperature and attendance, and three of four is a 25 percent cut nobody notices until the year is gone.
